The X2D, filaments and upkeep

The dual-nozzle toolhead

Launch coverage describes the X2D as a dual-nozzle machine with a mechanical switching mechanism (no extra motor in the toolhead). One nozzle is the main model nozzle; the second is mainly pitched for support material, including soluble supports, rather than for colour changes. In dual-nozzle mode the usable X width and height are reduced compared with the main nozzle alone — reviewers quote roughly 235 × 256 × 256 mm against 256 × 256 × 260 mm. Check the Bambu Wiki for your printer's current figures.

Why it matters for design: you can print complex overhangs with support material that comes off cleanly or dissolves, instead of fighting scarred surfaces. That rewards more ambitious geometry: internal channels, bridged cavities and nested parts.

Not verified here: exact Bambu Studio menu steps for assigning the second nozzle, which support-filament pairs are approved, and AMS compatibility. Read the Wiki page for your model; don't rely on forum guesses.

The heated chamber

The enclosure heats up to 65 °C. Hot chambers reduce warping and improve layer bonding in materials that shrink strongly (ABS, ASA, nylon, polycarbonate). Nozzles up to 300 °C cover most common engineering filaments.

PLA warning: PLA softens at low temperatures, and a hot chamber can soften the filament before the nozzle (heat creep). For PLA keep the chamber cool, usually by opening the door/lid as the Wiki recommends.

Choosing filament

MaterialStrengthsWatch out for
PLAEasy, stiff, great detailSoftens near 55–60 °C, brittle; keep out of cars
PETGTougher, better heat resistanceStringing; sticks strongly to some plates
ABS / ASAHeat and UV resistance (ASA), post-processingNeeds enclosure; fumes: ventilate
TPUFlexible partsSlow printing; often needs dry filament
PA (nylon) / PCStrong, tough, high heatAbsorb moisture: dry before printing
Carbon/glass fibre blendsStiff, dimensionally stableAbrasive: use a hardened nozzle

Reviews list a hardened-steel 0.4 mm nozzle on the X2D, which suits abrasive filaments, but confirm what your unit ships with.

  • Store filament sealed with desiccant. Dry nylon, TPU, PETG and PVA before printing.
  • Use the bundled filament profiles in Bambu Studio: they set temperature, cooling and flow.
  • Ventilate when printing ABS/ASA/PC.

Upkeep routine

  • Every print: check the first layer; wipe the plate with isopropyl alcohol or warm soapy water for adhesion.
  • Weekly: inspect the nozzle for clumps, clear debris from the purge area, check cables and fans.
  • Per the Wiki: follow the lubrication and calibration schedule for your printer. Don't invent intervals.
  • Keep a log: material, settings, result. After 20 entries you'll have your own cookbook.

Exercises

EXERCISE 1

Material choice

Choose filament for: (a) a car-dash phone mount in summer; (b) a quick visual prototype; (c) a flexible drone-camera mount; (d) a gear that sees friction and heat.

Hint
Think heat, stiffness, flexibility.
Show solution
(a) ASA or PETG (heat/UV). (b) PLA. (c) TPU. (d) Nylon (PA) or carbon-filled nylon, dried, with a hardened nozzle.
EXERCISE 2

Design for the support nozzle

Sketch (on paper) a part that would be hard with normal supports but easy with dissolvable ones.

Hint
Internal cavities are hard to reach.
Show solution
A box with a captive internal ball joint, or a hollow channel with overhanging roofs. After printing, soluble support washes out of spaces tools can't reach.

Start your log

Create a table with columns: date, file, material, layer/loops/infill, result, next change. Log your next print.

Hint
Use your notes box or a spreadsheet.
Show solution
Consistent logging beats memory. After a month, sort by result to see which settings work for you.

Checkpoint quiz

The X2D's second nozzle is mainly intended for…

It's pitched at (soluble) supports, not multicolour printing.

Why take care with PLA in a hot chamber?

Heat creep. Open the chamber door/lid for PLA as the Wiki advises.

Which filament absorbs moisture strongly and should be dried?

PA, TPU, PETG and PVA all benefit from drying.
