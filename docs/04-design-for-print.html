Heat-set inserts and self-tapping screws need specific hole sizes. Use the supplier's datasheet: for example, short M3 inserts commonly want a hole of about 4.0–4.2 mm, and a self-tapping M3 screw a pilot around 2.5 mm — but verify with a test coupon.

Orientation and splitting

  • Put the largest flat face on the plate: best adhesion, no support.
  • Run layers along the direction of load.
  • Put critical round holes vertical (axis along Z); horizontal holes print slightly oval and sag at the top. If horizontal is unavoidable, use a teardrop or diamond hole shape.
  • Split complex parts into pieces that each print flat, then join with pins, screws or glue.
  • Add chamfers on lower edges instead of overhanging fillets.

Worked example: M3 screw boss

You need a post that takes an M3 screw into a heat-set insert (4.2 mm hole from the datasheet). The post wall around the hole should be at least 2 mm: 4.2 + 2×2 = 8.2, so use an 8–9 mm post. Fillet its base 1 mm onto the floor so it doesn't snap off.

Exercises

EXERCISE 1

Size three holes

Pick CAD diameters for: a 5 mm steel rod that must slide freely, a 3 mm screw passing through, and a 10 mm magnet that should press in.

Hint
Use nominal + clearance from the table; press fits need a test.
Show solution
Rod: 5.3–5.4 mm. M3 screw: 3.3–3.4 mm. Magnet: start at 10.1 mm (+0.1), print a test piece, and adjust in 0.05 mm steps.
EXERCISE 2

Fix the design

A bracket has a shelf that leans 60° from vertical and a 0.8 mm wall. Propose two changes so it prints without supports and survives use.

Hint
One change handles the overhang, one the wall.
Show solution
Chamfer the shelf underside to 45° (or add a triangular gusset). Thicken the wall to at least 1.2 mm — 2.0 mm if it carries load.
EXERCISE 3

Orient it

A hinge pin 5 mm in diameter and 30 mm long will be bent by a lever. Lying flat or standing? Why?

Hint
Which orientation puts layer lines along the length?
Show solution
Lying flat: layers run along the pin so bending doesn't peel layers apart. Standing, it would snap at a layer line. Use a flat-bottomed profile or a tiny support if the round shape sags.

Checkpoint quiz

Minimum wall for a load-bearing part?

Load-carrying walls should be several loops thick, ≥ 2 mm.

An M3 clearance hole in CAD should be about…

Holes print small, so add 0.2–0.4 mm to the screw diameter.

Which gap makes a moving joint that doesn't fuse?

Below ~0.1 mm the lines merge; 0.3–0.4 mm slides freely.

Best shape for a horizontal round hole you can't rotate?

A teardrop's peak is 45°, so it prints without support.

Why chamfer the bottom edge?

The squished first layers bulge outward; a chamfer absorbs that.
