Capstone: a snap-fit box

By the end you can…

  • Design a two-part parametric box with a snap-fit lid
  • Calculate whether the snap hooks will survive
  • Print, measure, adjust and document the result

The brief

Design and print a small storage box (inner size about 60 × 40 × 25 mm) with a lid that snaps shut without screws. It must close firmly, open without breaking, print without supports, and be fully parametric so you can resize it for anything (SD cards, coins, spare screws).

Specification

  • Two bodies: box and lid, both with a flat face on the plate.
  • Named parameters: in_w, in_d, in_h, wall, clear, snap_t, snap_len, snap_y.
  • Walls ≥ 2 mm; all outside bottom edges chamfered 0.5 mm; inside corners filleted ≥ 1 mm.
  • Lid lip fits inside the box with clear = 0.25 mm per side.
  • Two cantilever snap hooks on the short sides.
  • Prints without supports.

Milestones

  1. M1 – Parameters and box body (module 3).
  2. M2 – Lid and lip with clearance (module 4).
  3. M3 – Snap hooks and strain check (below).
  4. M4 – Export 3MF in mm, slice in Bambu Studio, check the preview (modules 2 and 6).
  5. M5 – Print, measure, iterate until the fit is right. Log every change.

Snap-hook check

For a straight rectangular cantilever hook, the peak strain when the tip deflects by y is approximately:

strain = 1.5 * t * y / L**2     # t = beam thickness, y = tip deflection, L = beam length (same units)

Example: t = 1.5 mm, y = 0.6 mm, L = 12 mm → 1.5 × 1.5 × 0.6 / 144 ≈ 0.0094 = 0.94%. That is comfortable for PETG; PLA is brittle, so prefer lower strain or a longer beam. Longer, thinner beams flex more safely, and beams printed with layers along their length stay strong, so orient the hooks so layers run along them.

Reference solution

Show one reference design

Parameters: in_w 60, in_d 40, in_h 25, wall 2.0, clear 0.25, snap_t 1.5, snap_len 12, snap_y 0.6.

  1. Box outer = inner + 2×wall; extrude to in_h + wall. Shell open top. Fillet vertical outside edges 2 mm, inside corners 1 mm. Chamfer the bottom outside edge 0.5 mm.
  2. On both short walls, cut a rectangular window 8 mm wide near the rim and leave a catch ledge 0.6 mm deep (snap_y).
  3. Lid plate = outer size × 2 mm thick. Lip below it = inner size − 2×clear, 4 mm tall, wall 1.6 mm.
  4. On the lip's short sides add a flexible hook: beam thickness snap_t, length snap_len, end bump height snap_y with a 45° lead-in chamfer (easy closing) and a steeper back face (holds firmly).
  5. Export both bodies to one 3MF; print the lid upside-down so the plate is flat on the bed and the lip points up.
  6. First-print result to expect: if the lid is too loose, reduce clear to 0.2 or raise snap_y to 0.7; if it is too tight or the hooks crack, raise clear to 0.3 or lengthen snap_len to 14.

EXERCISE 2

Stress test

Open and close the lid 50 times. Where does it wear first? Redesign that spot.

Hint
Look at the hook root.
Show solution
Cracks at the hook root mean strain is too high. Lengthen the beam, thin it, or add a larger root fillet.
