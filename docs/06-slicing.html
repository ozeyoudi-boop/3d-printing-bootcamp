Slicing in Bambu Studio

By the end you can…

  • Walk the Prepare → Slice → Print workflow
  • Set quality, walls, infill and supports for a purpose
  • Read the preview to catch problems before wasting filament

The Bambu Studio flow

  1. Prepare tab: select your printer, plate type and filament. Import the model (File → Import, or drag-and-drop).
  2. Place and orient: R (rotate), S (scale), the lay-on-face tool, and the Cut and Support-paint tools. Check dimensions in the object info panel.
  3. Process settings: quality, strength, supports, others.
  4. Slice plate, then scrub through the layer slider in Preview.
  5. Print plate (send to printer) or export G-code/3MF.

The settings that matter most

SettingWhat it doesStarting point
Layer heightSurface smoothness vs time0.20 mm everyday; 0.12–0.16 for detail; 0.28 for quick drafts
Wall loopsStrength + stiffness (more than infill!)3 for general, 4–5 for functional
Top / bottom shell layersClosed skins≈ 0.8–1.2 mm worth of layers
Sparse infill densityInternal fill10–15% display, 20–30% for functional
Infill patternStrength vs timeGyroid, cubic or grid for general use
SupportsHolds overhangsTree supports for organic shapes; normal supports for flat undersides
BrimExtra first-layer adhesionOn for tall, thin or warping-prone parts
Seam positionWhere layers start/endHide it in a corner or behind a feature
Strength tip: walls carry most of the load. Going from 3 to 5 wall loops usually helps a functional part more than raising infill from 15% to 40%.
Angle conventions differ. This course measures overhangs as lean from vertical (45° limit). Slicers' “support threshold angle” is measured from horizontal (30° threshold ≈ 60° lean from vertical). Don't mix them up.

Worked example: slice the cable clip

  1. Import the clip 3MF. It should show your intended size (≈ 30 mm wide). If it's tiny, revisit module 2.
  2. Lay it on its flat base. Supports: off (the design avoids them).
  3. Layer 0.20 mm, 4 wall loops (functional clip), infill 20% gyroid.
  4. Slice. In Preview, step through layers and check the cable hole is round, the slot is open, and the first layer is a full footprint.
  5. Print one. Measure the hole with calipers. Is it cable_d + clear? If it is 0.2 mm small, add 0.2 to clear in CAD, re-export, repeat.

Reading the preview

  • Colour by Line type: look at walls, infill, support, bridges.
  • Scrub the layer slider through areas with overhangs: gaps under bridges or dense support clusters suggest orientation changes.
  • The estimate shows time and filament use. Compare two orientations before committing.

Exercises

EXERCISE 1

Time vs quality

Slice the same part at 0.12, 0.20 and 0.28 mm layer height. Record time and filament. Which would you use for a draft fit-check?

Hint
Look at the estimated time on the slice result.
Show solution
Use the 0.28 mm draft for fit-checks, 0.20 for general, 0.12 for display parts. Expect the 0.12 mm slice to take noticeably longer, often 2× or more compared with 0.28.
EXERCISE 2

Strength experiment

Print two small clips: one with 2 loops/40% infill, one with 5 loops/10% infill. Bend them to failure. Which is stronger and why?

Hint
Walls carry bending load.
Show solution
The 5-loop, 10%-infill clip usually wins: more material is at the outer edges, where bending stress is highest.
EXERCISE 3

Support or redesign?

Your part needs supports under a shelf that leans 60° from vertical. List two redesigns that avoid them.

Hint
Change the geometry, not the settings.
Show solution
Chamfer the shelf to 45°; split the part so the shelf prints flat; add a bridged arch under the shelf.
