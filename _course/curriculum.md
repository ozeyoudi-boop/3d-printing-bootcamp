# 3D Printing Bootcamp — Bambu Lab X2D (zero → hero)

Learner: motivated beginner who owns a Bambu Lab X2D ("XT2D" in the request) and wants to design, edit, convert, slice and print their own models.
Destination: design a functional part from scratch, repair/modify downloaded models, choose the right file format, slice it with sensible settings, and print it reliably.

| # | Slug | Promise |
|---|------|---------|
| 00 | orientation | Know the printer, the workflow, and the tool map |
| 01 | how-fdm-works | Understand why designs succeed or fail on an FDM printer |
| 02 | file-formats | Pick and convert STL / 3MF / STEP / OBJ correctly |
| 03 | design-first-model | Build a parametric part in Fusion 360 / Tinkercad |
| 04 | design-for-print | Apply tolerances, overhang, wall and orientation rules |
| 05 | edit-and-repair | Modify, repair and remix existing meshes (Blender, Bambu Studio, MakerWorld) |
| 06 | slicing | Bambu Studio: plate, orient, supports, walls, infill, speed |
| 07 | x2d-and-materials | Dual-nozzle workflow, filament and chamber choices, maintenance |
| — | capstone | Design, print and iterate a two-part snap-fit box |
| — | reference | Cheat sheet, glossary, troubleshooting, next steps |

Ordering rationale: physics before CAD (01), formats before tools (02) so conversion never confuses, CAD (03) before design rules (04), then editing (05) uses both, slicing (06) needs geometry knowledge, machine-specific (07) last.

Accuracy note: only X2D facts confirmed from launch coverage are stated as fact (dual nozzle with mechanical switching, heated chamber to 65 °C, nozzles to 300 °C, ~256×256×256–260 mm single-nozzle volume, smaller X travel with the second nozzle, Bambu Studio + MakerWorld). Everything else is generic FDM practice; the course tells the learner to check the Bambu Wiki for model-specific values.
